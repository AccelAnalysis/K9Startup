    Choose a service · bed bugs · about 3 minutes

    Bed bug detection

    Sell a clearly defined inspection that helps a customer locate possible activity and decide what to do next.

    Licensing and credentials

    • Ask the state pesticide or structural pest-control regulator whether paid canine inspection alone needs a company license, an individual credential, or supervision.
    • Do not assume that avoiding pesticide application creates an exemption.
    • If treatment is offered, confirm pesticide and business permissions separately. A referral does not authorize your own activity.
    • Use an independent dog-and-handler evaluation appropriate to bed bug work. NESDCA is one example. Confirm eligibility, target scope, renewal, and fees directly.

    Service and quality

    • Hotels and multifamily properties may need repeat inspections. Pest firms can be referral or subcontract partners.
    • Get written access authorization and site-preparation instructions. Record recent chemicals, inaccessible rooms, clutter, and limits.
    • Agree on alert confirmation with a qualified pest professional. Separate the dog alert, any visual evidence, and the recommended next step.
    • Contain training aids and keep them off client equipment. Stop aids and pests moving between sites.
    • Quote rooms or units, travel, preparation, reporting, confirmation, and return visits. Keep treatment charges separate.

    Report the locations searched, alerts observed, limitations, and confirmation findings. An unconfirmed alert is not proof of an infestation. A search without an alert does not establish that the entire property is pest-free.

    0:13–0:16 · about 3 minutes. Land the exemption myth and the report sentence. Those are the two lines people get wrong.

    • NESDCA is an example, not “the” license, and not a fee you should quote from memory. They confirm fees themselves.
    • If someone wants to treat, that is a different set of permissions.
    • NPMA best practices are the confirmation reference in the packet. Link is on the sources slides.

    Choose a service · mold · about 3 minutes

    Mold detection

    A canine alert can guide further investigation. It does not identify a species, measure exposure, establish a health diagnosis, or certify a building as safe.

    Licensing and boundaries

    • Texas, Florida, and New York are examples of states with mold licensing programs. Ask whether your search, advertising, sampling, report, or recommendations are regulated assessment. Company and individual requirements may differ.
    • Some states restrict the same provider from assessing and remediating the same property. New York expressly prohibits a licensee from performing both.
    • A private credential does not replace a state license. There is no basis in this packet for claiming government approval of a mold dog.

    What the customer receives

    • Record accessible areas, moisture evidence, alert locations, and what remains unknown. Name who will assess further and who may recommend remediation.
    • Sample only when a qualified professional determines it will answer a defined question. EPA notes that visible mold often does not require sampling, and that federal airborne mold thresholds have not been set.
    • Get written insurance confirmation for the actual service. Ask about pollution and mold exclusions, professional liability, and any required specialized coverage.

    Do not advertise a dog as determining toxic mold, diagnosing illness, proving compliance with a federal mold limit, or providing remediation clearance. Make the limits clear before a customer pays.

    0:16–0:19 · about 3 minutes. “Clearance” and “toxic mold” are the phrases to retire out loud.

    • Texas, Florida, and New York are examples, not a complete list. Every student still asks the regulator where they will work.
    • New York’s assess-and-remediate bar is specific. Other states: check conflict-of-interest rules before a combined service.
    • Insurance has to name the mold-related service. A general policy with a mold exclusion is not “covered.”

    Choose a service · narcotics · about 3 minutes

    Private narcotics detection

    Private detection work needs a defined legal basis for access and a customer-approved response plan. A canine business is not a law-enforcement agency.

    Before accepting searches

    • Property-owner permission alone may not resolve employee, tenant, student, or other third-party rights.
    • Have counsel review search authority, consent, workplace and school policies, privacy, reporting, and notification. Ask whether private-security or investigative licensing applies to the company and the handlers.
    • State exactly which target odors the team is trained and evaluated to detect. Discuss cannabis where client policy or state law makes it relevant.
    • Agree who receives an alert report and who decides next steps. Do not describe an alert as proof of possession, impairment, a crime, or the identity of a particular substance.

    Training aids and credentials

    • Before acquiring or possessing controlled-substance training aids, contact DEA Diversion and the state controlled-substance regulator. DEA lists canine handlers under Form 225. Confirm category, schedules, premises, storage, records, fees, and lawful supply.
    • Do not assume another party’s registration covers your possession, transport, or storage.
    • Synthetic or alternative aids do not remove the need to demonstrate operational detection. Validate the team through lawful, authorized arrangements.
    • Choose a certification the intended clients accept. NAPWDA, USPCA, and NNDDA differ. Confirm private-handler acceptance directly.

    Limit identifying information. Keep secure reports and an agreed retention schedule. Route discovered substances and safety concerns through the customer’s response plan. Do not handle unknown materials.

    0:19–0:22 · about 3 minutes. The alert-is-not-proof line and the “someone else’s registration” line are the ones to pause on.

    • Do not invent a search script. Counsel reviews authority, consent, and notification.
    • Form 225 is the DEA listing the packet names. Students confirm category, schedules, premises, storage, records, and fees. Do not quote a fee.
    • Cannabis is a discussion item where policy or state law makes it relevant, not a slogan.

    Choose a service · explosives · about 3 minutes

    Explosives detection

    Plan time and capital for authorization, insurance, independent evaluation, and ongoing training before you promise a deployment date.

    Authorization and safety first

    • Ask ATF which license or permit, if any, is needed for your proposed acquisition, possession, and use of regulated explosive training aids. Also check state and local fire, zoning, and security requirements.
    • ATF storage depends on the materials and the circumstances. Get a compliant storage plan. Do not pick a magazine type from a student guide. Confirm transport separately.
    • Instruction and handling only through qualified, authorized providers. Do not assume non-detonable or commercially packaged aids are exempt.

    Standards and customer programs

    • ANSI/ASB Standard 092 addresses training and certification of explosives detection teams. Read the current standard and the customer’s acceptance rules. A standard is not itself a license.
    • Air cargo screening is a separate TSA program. A general explosives credential does not authorize cargo screening. Team certification and provider registration under the applicable CCSF-K9 framework have to be addressed.
    • DHS SAFETY Act protections require a qualifying approval and apply only within that scope. They are not an insurance policy and not automatic protection for every explosives team. Review with counsel and your insurer.

    Agree on search scope, handoff, and the client’s emergency plan. Do not promise bomb-free clearance. A suspected explosive threat requires the designated public-safety response. A private canine team is not a bomb-disposal service.

    0:22–0:25 · about 3 minutes. Push the commercial starting point: subcontract under an established qualified provider before building independent infrastructure.

    • Do not name a magazine type. The packet refuses that shortcut.
    • Standard 092, TSA cargo, and the SAFETY Act are three different things. None of them is a general license to deploy.
    • “If any” matters. Students ask ATF about their exact materials and activities. They do not assume a permit is required or that none is.

    Validate demand · about 4 minutes

    Validate demand before you invest

    National market statistics are not enough to demonstrate demand in your service area. Use buyer conversations to find a recurring problem, the purchasing authority, and what makes a provider acceptable.

    A practical first cycle

    • Select one buyer segment and one travel area. Example: pest management firms within a practical driving radius, not every property owner in the state.
    • As a starting target, speak with 10 prospective buyers and compare at least three alternatives.
    • These are suggested research targets, not proof that a market exists.

    What to learn

    • How they handle the problem today.
    • When they last paid for a related service.
    • Who approves the purchase.
    • What evidence or credentials they require.
    • Test a real offer: scope, deliverable, scheduling, and estimated price. Track objections and any willingness to consider a paid pilot once you are authorized and ready.

    Price and cash · about 3 minutes

    Price for sustainable delivery

    Set prices from your full delivery cost, local buyer expectations, and service value. A published price from another market can omit confirmation, travel, preparation, or reporting. It is not a forecast for your business.

    Build every quote from the same inputs

    • Search units or area, complexity, travel, minimum callout, site preparation, waiting time, reporting, confirmation, reinspection, after-hours work, and applicable taxes.
    • Count owner time for travel, setup, administration, and reporting.
    • Reserve capacity and budget for maintenance training, dog rest, equipment, and replacement.
    • Separate direct job costs from fixed overhead. Do not count the same owner labor or expense twice.

    Planning formulas

    • Job contribution = job revenue − job-variable costs.
    • Break-even jobs = monthly fixed operating costs ÷ average job contribution, rounded up.
    • For an owner-income target, add desired owner compensation to fixed costs only if it is not already included.
    • Taxes, debt payments, and reserves need their own cash plan.

    1:02–1:05 · about 3 minutes. The formulas are on the next two slides with numbers. Here, make sure “variable” and “fixed” are different piles.

    • Owner time is easy to forget and easy to double-count. Say both warnings.
    • Dog rest is a capacity constraint, not a slogan. It shows up again in welfare and in the 25-job illustration.

    Deliver the work · about 3 minutes

    Deliver a consistent service

    Use one workflow for every assignment. Record who owns each step. Stop when authorization, safety, or team readiness is unresolved.

    Seven-step service workflow
    StepRequired output
    1. Qualify the inquiryTarget, buyer, site, access authority, need, requested timing, and fit.
    2. Quote and authorizeWritten scope, exclusions, price, access permission, client response responsibilities, and acceptance.
    3. Prepare the visitSite instructions, hazards, contacts, lawful aid handling if relevant, and a transport plan.
    4. Confirm readinessCurrent team credentials, dog fitness, equipment, rest plan, and insurance or client requirements.
    5. Conduct the agreed searchLocations and conditions recorded. Inaccessible areas and stop decisions documented.
    6. Report and hand offSeparate alerts, confirmation, limitations, and actions. Deliver securely to authorized recipients.
    7. Invoice and follow upSend the invoice, verify receipt, track collection, schedule any agreed next service, and capture feedback.

    Pause or decline if you lack permission, required credentials, suitable conditions, or a fit dog-and-handler team. A documented limit or a declined job protects trust better than an unsupported clearance claim.

    Deliver the work · about 3 minutes

    Agreements and the minimum report

    Agreement topics for counsel

    • Named customer and contracting entity; who can authorize access; exact sites and search areas; target odors; exclusions; preparation duties.
    • Deliverable and confirmation; limits of canine detection; who responds to an alert; emergency handoff.
    • Price, minimum charge, deposit if appropriate, payment due date, changes, waiting time, cancellation, and return visits.
    • Insurance and client requirements; confidentiality; data access and retention; dispute terms; enforceable liability provisions.

    Minimum service report

    • Job and team. Unique job ID, client, date and time, handler, dog, certification scope and expiry.
    • Scope and conditions. Authorized target odors, areas searched, access restrictions, and conditions that affected the search.
    • Observations. Alert locations and relevant objective observations. Avoid unsupported conclusions.
    • Confirmation. Method, person responsible, findings, or “not confirmed,” separate from the alert.
    • Handoff and limitations. Recipient, time, agreed action, areas not searched, and limitations.
    • Attachments and control. Approved photos or maps, author, version, secure storage location, and retention category.

    Example wording to adapt, then have counsel review: “The team searched the areas listed. An alert was observed at [location]. Confirmation status: [finding / not confirmed]. This report describes the search conditions and observations; it does not guarantee the absence of target material elsewhere.”

    1:21–1:24 · about 3 minutes. Read the example sentence. Tell them to adapt it to the niche and have counsel review it. It is not a form they can photocopy into a contract today.

    Deliver the work · about 3 minutes

    Quality, records, and dog welfare

    Certification is one checkpoint in an ongoing quality system. A universal accuracy percentage cannot be assumed from graduation or certification.

    A manageable quality routine

    • Keep initial and current certification scope, dates, evaluator, target list, and conditions. Set reminders before renewal.
    • Training records: known targets and blank searches, aid identity, environment, results, and corrective actions. Use qualified independent or blinded evaluation when practical.
    • Keep target-present training results separate from real-job alerts whose truth may be unknown. Do not calculate field accuracy from unconfirmed alerts.
    • Review complaints, missed commitments, confirmation outcomes, and repeat work. Investigate the cause and document the correction.

    Dog care and capacity

    • Plan veterinary care, conditioning, hydration, heat protection, safe transport, rest, and criteria for stopping a search. Use veterinary and qualified trainer guidance for individual limits.

    Check: alerts and accuracy

    1. A canine alert proves the target material is present and identifies the substance or the person responsible.

    No. Do not describe an alert as proof of possession, impairment, a crime, or the identity of a particular substance. Separate the alert, the confirmation, and the limits. An unconfirmed bed-bug alert is not proof of an infestation. A mold alert does not certify a building safe. An explosives search is not a bomb-free clearance.

    2. Graduation or a certification lets you publish a field-accuracy percentage.

    No. A universal accuracy percentage cannot be assumed from graduation or certification. Do not calculate field accuracy from unconfirmed alerts. Real-job alerts may have an unknown truth. Keep them separate from target-present training outcomes.

    Funding that fits the business

    Match funding to what you are buying and how the business will repay it. Confirm demand and permissions before you sign financing or commit to major assets.

    SBIR and STTR, only for real R&D

    SBIR and STTR support eligible small businesses doing research and development with commercialization potential. Routine service delivery, or buying a dog and a vehicle, is not an R&D project.

    Phase I

    Feasibility and technical merit of the proposed research.

    Phase II

    Further research and development based on qualifying results and agency rules.

    Phase III

    Commercialization using non-SBIR/STTR funding, sales, investment, or other contracts.

    • Name the technical uncertainty, the experiment, the potential customer, and the measurable result. Ordinary recordkeeping software without meaningful technical novelty may not qualify.
    • Check the solicitation for ownership, employee count, principal investigator, location, work share, and submission rules. STTR requires an eligible research-institution partnership, with minimum work-share requirements.
    • Look at DoD, DHS, USDA, NIH, NSF, DOE, and other agencies only where the idea matches the mission. Use sbir.gov/topics and verify the live solicitation.
    • Start SAM.gov / UEI and the required agency and SBA registrations early. Budget authorized research costs, not generic overhead.

    The packet notes that SBIR/STTR were reauthorized in April 2026 through September 30, 2031. Each current solicitation controls award limits, deadlines, and eligibility. A general national dollar ceiling is not an offer or a funding promise.
